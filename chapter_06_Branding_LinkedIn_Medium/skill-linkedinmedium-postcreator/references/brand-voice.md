## 7. Heat curve and tone dial

In these packs, the **hook** is allowed to be provocative. Vineet asked for controversial hooks, and they are the scroll-stopper. The **body** goes back to his normal tone dial: 70% steady mentor, 20% blunt, 10% self-deprecating. The **close** is kind.

The body should never sound angry or smug. The blunt part aims at decisions ("five courses and finish none"), never at people.

## 9. Signature language

**Line bank.** These are original lines built on his work. Use at most one per piece, usually as the Lesson or the Medium pull quote.

- "Automation doesn't replace judgment. It buys you time to use it."
- "Flaky tests don't fail. They train your team to stop reading failures."
- "A pass rate you don't trust is just a slower way of saying 'we don't know.'"
- "Five days of regression isn't thoroughness. It's a queue."
- "When you're the only QA, 'the team' means you."
- "I don't sign off on builds. I sign off on risks I can name."
- "Go/no-go is not a feeling. It's three numbers and one conversation."
- "The platform dies. The testing instinct doesn't."
- "Shift-left isn't a meeting. It's asking the awkward question before the code exists."
- "Salesforce updates your org three times a year. Your test suite doesn't get a vote."
- "Your manual experience isn't what's holding you back. It's your head start."

**Sign-off.** Every PS asks one direct question tied to the post, then lowers the bar with an admission, ending in **"so no judgment here."** Repeated over time, this becomes recognisably his.

- "PS: Which step are you stuck on right now? I started manual too, so no judgment here."
- "PS: What's your pipeline pass rate right now? I've been at 70, so no judgment here."

**Recurring motifs.** The sign-off moment. Being the only QA in the room. The before → after number. Working US and UK release hours from Delhi. Fail → pass (red → green), which is also the color logic of his images.

## 10. Style rules

- Write in first person singular ("I"), talking to "you".
- Use past tense for the story and present tense for the lesson.
- Keep lines mostly short, with one longer sentence per section that carries the reasoning. If every line is a one-liner, it reads like a template.
- Write numbers as digits: "10 exercises", "90 days", "5 days → 3 hours".
- Use → arrows for steps and moves, and ○ circles for stat lists.
- Use at most one "isn't X, it's Y" construction per LinkedIn post, and no more than three per Medium article.
- Avoid three-adjective lists, rhetorical questions stacked back to back, and hashtags inside sentences.

## 11. Banned words and moves

- "Thrilled / delighted to share", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "leverage", "unlock", "delve", "seamless", "journey" (for a career)
- "Agree?" as a standalone call to action
- Other creators' signatures: "Real answers only", "Real numbers only", "rules, not vibes", "a hope with a green checkmark", "QA is getting a package manager"
- "QA is dead" framing, and "Nobody talks about this" clickbait
- Emojis, bold unicode text, and "comment YES and I'll DM you" bait
- Mocking manual testers, developers, juniors or managers as people

## 12. How he differs from the creators who inspired the format

The format came from sample posts Vineet admired, mostly Pramod Dutta's: two-line hooks, arrow lists, short lines, PS questions, and an X-style card with a blue highlight. Borrow the mechanics. Don't borrow the identity.

- Pramod's identity is the builder-educator: AI-first, big community numbers.
- Vineet's edge is ownership and depth: a decade of real release calls, in enterprise CRM and healthcare, where most QA content never goes.
- Visually, Vineet's accent colors are red and green (fail → pass), never the blue highlight.
